Keypad walkthrough

Every tile on these keypads was drawn by the plugin's own renderer.

Step 1

Install with one command

It checks what you need, builds the plugin, wires the Claude Code hooks and the status line, loads it into Logi Plugin Service - and confirms it came up.

  • Safe to re-run. Every step is idempotent.
  • Reversible. ./install.sh --uninstall puts your settings back exactly.
  • Look first. ./install.sh --check changes nothing.
❯ brew install dotnet
❯ git clone <this repo>
❯ cd mx-keypad-warp-claude-code && ./install.sh

Step 2

Put the keys on your keypad

In Logi Options+ open your MX Creative Keypad, find MX Keypad Warp Claude Code in the actions panel, and drag onto the empty keys:

  • Overview, Next, Allow - group Claude
  • Session slot 1 … 4 - group Session slots
  • Claude Sessions - a folder, group Claude
  • App Switcher - a folder, group Apps
Step 3

The main page is the cockpit

It tells you which session wants you, not just how many.

  • Overview - every session as a square in its state colour, under a headline for the most urgent thing.
  • Next - the one session that most needs you, in full. Press to go there.
  • Slots - your sessions, live. Press to jump to the pane; hold to interrupt.
  • Allow - the pending command, spelled out. Press to approve; hold to look.
Step 3, later

…and quiet when nothing needs you

No red, no blinking. A working tile turns a spinner and runs a wave along its edge; a finished one shows how long ago and on which model.

  • coral working · green your turn · red blocked on you
  • purple errored · amber out of usage · grey idle
  • The bar on top is the context window filling up.
Step 4

Open Claude Sessions

Five sessions a page. A sixth starts page two, and the bottom row comes with it.

  • session - your five-hour window: how much is used, when it resets.
  • weekly - all models, this week.
  • pace - at this rate, do you reach the reset? It says where you land, or how long until you run out.
  • Side bars mark the pane you are actually typing in.
Step 5

Press a session: its own page

Everything about one session, and everything about it you can change.

  • info - context in % and tokens, branch, turns, age.
  • model · effort · mode - tap to step through the choices.
  • The tile - press to jump to its pane, hold to interrupt it.
  • ‹ sessions - back up, with a dot for every other session. Hold it to go straight to the one that needs you most.
Step 6

Answer a prompt from the keypad

A blocked tile shows the command it wants to run. Its answers come first on its page.

  • yes · always · no - typed into that exact pane, and only if the prompt is still open.
  • Guarded. Keys refuse to type unless the right terminal is in front.
  • ‹ sessions turns red and names the session when another one needs you.
Step 6, also

Multiple choice: the real options

When Claude asks a question, the tile shows the question and the keys show its actual answers.

  • They appear only when every option fits - three of four would be worse than none.
  • One question at a time; anything more involved is left to the terminal.
Step 7

Tap to change, commit when you stop

Each tap steps to the next choice, ringed in white. The bar runs down; when it is empty the change is sent - once.

  • high → xhigh → max is one /effort max, not three commands.
  • Tap all the way round to where you started and nothing is sent.
  • Same gesture for model and permission mode.
Step 8

App Switcher: a Cmd-Tab you can see

Press an app and it comes forward; the folder closes itself.

  • Pinned apps never move - Warp first by default. The rest are most-recently-used, frozen while the folder is open.
  • The badge is your Claude sessions in that terminal: red 1 means one is blocked on you.
  • White bar = in front. Dimmed = hidden. Hold to hide an app.
